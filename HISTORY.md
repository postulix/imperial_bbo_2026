# BBO Capstone: How the Search Progressed

## Overview

The challenge was to maximise eight unknown black-box functions (2D to 8D), submitting one query per function per week. Each function began with 10 to 40 initial observations and gained 12 weekly observations (rounds R1 to R12), after which a final-round query was prepared for each.

**Shared approach.** Every function was handled in its own notebook, reading one harmonised dataset (`data/data.csv`). The common ingredients were:

- Gaussian process (GP) surrogates with ARD kernels (RBF and Matérn), with target transformations (Yeo-Johnson, log, signed-log) where the response spanned orders of magnitude.
- Acquisition functions (posterior mean, EI, PI, UCB) compared against each other.
- TuRBO-style trust regions, small neural-network ensembles and consensus rankings across models, as challengers to a single GP.
- Leave-one-out cross-validation and conditional profiles to check each model before trusting it.

Early rounds relied on a single GP and one acquisition function. As data grew, different kernels and settings produced different recommendations, and the process shifted toward comparing models and looking for agreement. The final query was always chosen by hand from the options each notebook prints. For the final round, three functions added an explicit last-round decision step: Function 2 took the local EI optimum after a lower R12 result, Function 6 added a calibration check and a model vote between two candidates, and Function 7 interpolated a quadratic through its last three rounds.

**Lessons that recurred.**

- *Check the structure before searching.* Function 8 was solved exactly by a 17-parameter quadratic fit that could have been made from the initial data. Linear screens and ARD length scales had marked its two most important inputs as irrelevant: a symmetric peak looks like zero correlation. Diagnostics on data already held are free; evaluations are not.
- *Be willing to change the modelling approach.* Function 8 also demonstrated the value of approaching a problem from a fundamentally different perspective. Rather than continuing to refine the Gaussian process model, treating the function as a regression problem revealed its underlying structure and identified the optimum. The lesson was not that regression is inherently better than GP, but that different methods can reveal patterns that others miss. When progress stalls, reconsider the approach rather than simply refining the existing model.
- *"Unidentified" is not "irrelevant".* A long fitted length scale only says a variable was not varied where the objective was high.
- *Simpler often won.* Additional model sophistication did not buy proportionate gains. A fully automated pipeline once introduced errors that sent results backwards for a week; refactoring and simplifying fixed it.
- *Check calibration, not just fit.* A walk-forward check on Function 6 (refit on earlier rounds, predict the next) showed the GP was overconfident: standardised errors reached +4.0 at R9 and −5.1 at R12.
- *Noise matters.* Functions 2, 3 and 6 had demonstrable observation noise (repeated or near-repeated inputs gave different outputs), so noise was estimated or fixed explicitly and single observations were not over-read.
- *Validate new capabilities.* LLMs were used to analyse diagnostics and propose models, but recommendations had to be supported by the data, checked in stages, and sometimes reviewed by a second model.
- *Process errors happen.* In Week 8 a Function 2 input was submitted to Function 1 (identical coordinates), costing that round.

## Function 1 (2D): local refinement of a sparse, extreme-range signal

Function 1 behaves like a proximity sensor: nearly everywhere returns values indistinguishable from zero, and the data spans more than 120 orders of magnitude. The first observation of any use was in R4 (1.4e-9), and R5 reached 7.2e-8, a 50-fold step that showed the signal could be followed. A local signed-log GP with an RBF kernel then guided a run of steps along a near-straight line: R6 3.04e-6, R7 3.26e-6, R9 3.41e-6, R10 3.50e-6 and R11 3.55e-6 (about 17% above R6), with diminishing returns each step. R8 was lost to the wrong-function submission. In Week 11 a quadratic fit, the local GP and a PI search agreed on a maximum along the trajectory near (0.67155, 0.70077). Because the good points lay on one narrow line, Week 12 tested a small step (0.0005) perpendicular to it, where the GP predicted a higher value with much greater uncertainty. It paid off: R12 returned 4.02e-6 at `0.671773-0.700322`, a new best about 13% above R11, suggesting the maximum lies off the established line. The notebook's candidate for the final round repeats the perpendicular step from the new incumbent: `0.671996-0.699874`.

## Function 2 (2D): a noisy function with several high-scoring regions

Function 2 is noisy: results varied between nearby inputs by more than a smooth surface would explain, so the models carried an explicit noise term, and it has at least two high regions. R1 returned about 0, but R2 found 0.589 in a second region near (0.70, 0.25), and R4 reached 0.648 near the original peak at (0.69, 0.87). Rounds 5 to 9 alternated between the two regions (R6 to R9 at x2 ≈ 0.275 to 0.30 reached 0.62 to 0.64) without beating the incumbent. R10 moved to a different area altogether, around x1 ≈ 0.51, and R11 improved it to 0.749 at (0.507, 0.55), the best of the run. A Matérn 3/2 ARD GP with a fixed noise floor consistently ranked x1 as the key variable (length scale 0.05) and treated x2 as effectively irrelevant (length scale 100). R12 probed x1 locally at 0.515 with x2 held at 0.55 and returned 0.605, below the R11 best. For the final round the search turns to the other side, taking the local EI optimum at lower x1 with x2 still held at 0.55: `0.494947-0.550000`. The function is noisy, so a single lower result is read with caution.

## Function 3 (3D): a noisy function with one well-identified input

Function 3 is a noisy drug-interaction surface with all outputs negative. Its first incumbent (R1, −0.0125) was re-evaluated in R5 and returned −0.027, direct evidence of noise. A Yeo-Johnson transform clearly improved leave-one-location-out accuracy, and two GP surrogates (RBF and Matérn 3/2) were kept; both found x3 the only well-identified dimension. R6 deliberately explored the region between the incumbent and the surrogates' preferred x3 ≈ 0, landing on a new best (−0.0082). R9 set the final incumbent (−0.00717 at about (0.63, 0.44, 0.44)). The follow-up, R10 (−0.019), came from almost the same place and contradicted it, and both models revised their predictions downward, so the push to higher x1 was abandoned. Week 12 returned toward the centre of the region, where posterior-mean optimisation, a Pareto analysis and a corridor search all converged: `0.565436-0.475753-0.431480`. Neither model predicted it would beat the incumbent. R12 returned −0.0082, close to the incumbent (−0.0072) and above R10 and R11, within the observed noise. After refitting with R12, the same consensus corridor search proposes `0.522256-0.468147-0.429676` for the final round.

## Function 4 (4D): a small positive region among mostly negative results

Function 4 is mostly strongly negative (only 9 of 42 observations are positive). R1 (−0.137) was one coordinate away from the first positive result: R4 changed x2 by 0.03 and gave +0.310, showing high local sensitivity. R5 (0.299) confirmed the basin was real. A small neural network was used as a challenger to the GP, which appeared too smooth for so narrow a ridge, and its candidate gave R6 (0.377). R8, at a round-number point (0.42, 0.36, 0.36, 0.42), produced the best value, 0.7308, and R10 (0.725) stayed close to it. Later rounds moved toward a model-comparison framework: a Matérn 3/2 GP, a neural-network ensemble and a TuRBO challenger scored the same candidates, with an SVM classifier tested and retired because every candidate passed. R11's NN-backed proposal returned only 0.199, so the primary GP was reinstated. Week 12 used the GP posterior-mean point `0.408162-0.339047-0.362578-0.412557`, which returned 0.490: positive, but well below the R8 best (0.731), consistent with the narrow region and the GP's large predictive uncertainty there. After refitting, the GP posterior-mean candidate for the final round is a small step toward the incumbent: `0.422624-0.350863-0.359345-0.422000`.

## Function 5 (4D): an optimum at the edge of the input range

Function 5 is a yield surface spanning about 0.1 to 8,700, which needed a log transform. From R1 the search ran toward the upper boundary (1,410 at R1, 3,290 at R3, 4,477 at R5). A log-log fit on the corner observations (R² = 0.9995) showed that x2, x3 and x4 had no headroom left, so the decision came down to x1, which had never been sampled above 0.37 near the optimum. R6 went to the corner (1, 1, 1, 1) and returned 8,662.48, nearly double the previous best. R7 to R9 probed just inside the corner (0.995, 0.99999, 0.999998), approaching the same value from below, and R10, R11 and R12 repeated the corner exactly. A log-target ARD GP reached a leave-one-out R² of 0.9998 and predicted 8,662.8 against 8,662.5 observed. Final submission: `1.000000-1.000000-1.000000-1.000000`, the upper boundary.

## Function 6 (5D): a noisy function with a late first positive result

Function 6 (a cake recipe, scored negative by design) is noisy: the GP's own noise estimate was about 0.05 on a scale where early values ran from −1.2 to −0.3. R5 reached −0.278 with a conservative interior candidate. R6 deliberately accepted a lower predicted mean in exchange for higher expected improvement, landing at −0.305; R7 then reached −0.237, and R9 −0.068 at (0.415, 0.365, 0.60, 0.78, 0.10). A rebuilt TuRBO-1 pipeline, centred on the best observed point and using a Matérn 5/2 ARD GP with an explicit noise term, then produced R11 = +0.0060, the first positive value. Week 12 followed the pipeline's posterior-mean and EI candidate, `0.444147-0.417600-0.736303-0.723905-0.012236` (predicted gain about 0.044), but R12 returned −0.348, far below R11. A walk-forward calibration check (refit on the earlier rounds, predict the next) shows the GP's uncertainty was too small, with standardised errors of +4.0 at R9 and −5.1 at R12. The final-round pipeline therefore constrains the GP (length scales bounded to 0.05–2.0 and a noise floor, so no input goes flat and noise cannot collapse) and compares two local candidates under four GP specifications: a conservative posterior-mean point within ±0.01 of the incumbent (A) and a maximum-probability-of-improvement point within ±0.04 (B). All four specifications prefer A, so the final-round candidate is `0.435845-0.385066-0.675783-0.748318-0.047503`.

## Function 7 (6D): steady improvement through widening local searches

Function 7 (hyperparameters of an ML model) climbed steadily: 1.36 at the best initial point, 2.31 at R1, 2.38 at R4, 2.39 at R5, then 2.42 at R6, 2.83 at R7, 3.24 at R9 and 3.28 at R11. By R5 raw and log GPs agreed the local basin was nearly exhausted (+0.35% from R4), and a TuRBO-1 pipeline took over. Replaying the history, its reconstructed trust-region length doubled from 0.2 to 0.4 after the R4 to R6 successes and reached its 0.5 cap after R9, consistent with the large later gains. A gradient-boosted-tree challenger was rejected in Week 11 because its apparent improvements disagreed with the GP and its cross-validation errors were high. Week 12 followed the posterior-mean candidate `0.235398-0.113038-0.510070-0.266307-0.308900-0.645262` but returned 3.273, slightly below the R11 best (3.283), the first failure in the replayed trust-region history. With the refitted GP now predicting a small decline from the R11 point, the final submission uses quadratic interpolation instead: a parabola through the equally spaced R10, R11 and R12 points (curvature −0.0129) has its vertex about a tenth of a step from R11 toward R12, predicting only +0.00015 over the best. The final-round candidate is `0.230117-0.105291-0.483789-0.263529-0.313756-0.650578`.

## Function 8 (8D): a function that could be fitted exactly

Function 8 improved slowly through successive rounds of search (9.80 at R1, 9.955 at R3, and 9.92–9.93 at R4 and R5). However, subsequent analysis revealed that the underlying function could have been identified from the outset by treating it as a regression problem rather than relying on iterative search. A 17-parameter quadratic regression (incorporating linear and squared terms for each input) fitted all 45 observations with negligible differences attributable to rounding in the original data. The model recovered simple, round-number coefficients: y = 9.1983 + Σ(bᵢxᵢ − aᵢxᵢ²), with an interior optimum at (0.10, 0.15, 0.13, 0.15, 0.80, 0.50, 0.20, 0.60) and a maximum of exactly 10. Final submission: `0.100000-0.150000-0.130000-0.150000-0.800000-0.500000-0.200000-0.600000`
